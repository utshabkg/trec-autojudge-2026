\section{Results on the Pilots}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:pilots} gives the current TIRA scores on rag25-gen and ragtime25, and
Table~\ref{tab:dragun} gives them on dragun-repgen. The field median and best are
over every entry on each leaderboard, read on 2026-10-19: 385 entries on rag25-gen,
206 on ragtime25 and 139 on dragun-repgen. Many entries are variants from the same
team, so a rank among entries is only a loose guide. The length row is the
organizers' baseline judge, which scores each response by its length.

The row ``rank only'' re-aggregates the exact judgments of the Qwen3-30B row with
\texttt{NR\_RANK} and nothing else. Our test-set system \run{nuggetrecall-rank}
emits both from the same judgments, so on the pilots its equivalent is the better
of the two rows in each column. The pilot entries on TIRA that carry the name
\run{nuggetrecall-rank} are separate re-runs, which Section~\ref{sec:noise} uses to
measure noise. The ensemble row emits a single fused measure
(Section~\ref{sec:ensemble}). The two \judge{Corroborate} floors are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:floor}.

On short answers \judge{NuggetRecall} sits near the field median. With gpt-oss-120b
it reaches 0.839 Kendall and 0.710 \tauap\ on Strict Vital Score, 20th of 385
entries on \tauap. \judge{Corroborate} stays between 0.26 and 0.32 there. On long
reports the better of our two judges depends on the truth measure.
\judge{NuggetRecall} is our best on Nugget Coverage and \judge{Corroborate} on F1,
where its \tauap\ is 32nd of 206 entries (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). On
dragun-repgen, \judge{NuggetRecall} with gpt-oss-120b is 4th of 139 entries on
Kendall and \judge{Corroborate} is 8th.
